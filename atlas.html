Subjects atlas

Every subject studied, grouped into eight disciplines, and the parts of the attitude-control project it feeds. Use it to see the big picture: which ideas from which subjects hold up which part of the spacecraft.

How to read the atlas

A link joins one subject to one of the thirteen project parts (the modules on the learning path). Each link carries three things:

  • A strength. Strong: the part depends on an idea or method from the subject. Moderate: a supporting idea. Weak: a useful but minor connection.
  • A tag. Most links are direct. Analogy marks a teaching bridge from another field; Extension marks a link to work beyond the original project, such as the sensors and estimation module.
  • A concept: one sentence saying exactly which idea crosses over.

Each subject also shows its evidence. Notes means its own notes support the links. Typical content means only the subject outline is available, so the listed topics are the standard syllabus for a subject of that name, not a record of what was taught. Partial notes sits in between. The full key is on the About page.

In the Disciplines view the heat table counts subjects per cell. The “strongest on” line under each discipline instead ranks parts by a weighted score, which sums the links of a discipline \(d\) into each project part \(m\) with a weight per strength:

\[ S_{d,m} \;=\; \sum_{c\,\in\,d} w(c,m) \]

where the weight of one link from subject \(c\) to part \(m\) is

\[ w(c,m) = \begin{cases} 3 & \text{strong} \\ 2 & \text{moderate} \\ 1 & \text{weak} \\ 0 & \text{no link} \end{cases} \]

The weights are a choice made for this site Site default. A discipline is “strongest on” the three parts with the highest \(S_{d,m}\), with ties kept, listed from the highest score down.
